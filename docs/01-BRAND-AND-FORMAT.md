# The Palmetto Beat — Brand & Issue Format

## Positioning

**One line:** Everything happening in Palmetto, in a five-minute read, three mornings a week.

**The wedge:** Every existing outlet covers *Manatee County* from Bradenton's side of the river. Palmetto is always the second item in someone else's story. The Palmetto Beat is the only publication where Palmetto is the whole story.

**Who it's for:** People who live, work, worship, or own a business in Palmetto and Ellenton — and want to actually know what's going on in their own city without reading a county-wide paper or scrolling a chaotic Facebook group.

**What it is not:** Not a hot-take politics newsletter. Not a churchy newsletter. Not a link dump. The tone is a well-informed neighbor who went to the commission meeting so you didn't have to.

## Voice rules

- Write like you're telling a friend at the coffee shop. Short sentences. Plain words.
- Always answer "so what does this mean for me?" — a rezoning is a story about traffic on your street.
- Neutral on politics, warm on community. You report what the commission decided, not whether they were right.
- Name real people and real businesses constantly. Local media lives or dies on how many people see their own name.
- Never bury the lede. First sentence of every item carries the news.
- No filler. If a section has nothing worth reading, cut the section that day.

## Name and identity

- **Publication:** The Palmetto Beat
- **Subject line prefix:** none — subject lines are the lead story itself
- **Sign-off:** your name, consistent every issue. A local newsletter needs a face.
- **Handle to claim everywhere:** `thepalmettobeat` (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, X, Threads)
- **Domain:** launching on the beehiiv subdomain. Register `thepalmettobeat.com` before you announce publicly — it costs ~$12 and you do not want to lose it once the name is out.

## Cadence

**Start: Tuesday / Thursday, 6:00 AM.**

Do not start daily. Local newsletters die from burnout, not from lack of demand. Two issues a week is sustainable while you also sell sponsorships and hold a job. Move to Mon/Wed/Fri once you have a repeatable sourcing routine, and only then consider daily.

6:00 AM matters — local newsletters are read with coffee. Schedule the send, never send manually.

## The issue format

Same skeleton every single issue. Readers should be able to find what they want without thinking, and you should never face a blank page.

---

### 1. The Lead (150-250 words)
The one thing that actually matters today. City commission decision, a development, a school issue, a business opening or closing, a crime story with real impact.

Structure: what happened → what it means for residents → what happens next and when.

### 2. Quick Hits (4-6 bullets, one or two sentences each)
Everything else worth knowing. Road closures, county actions, water restrictions, permit filings, sports results, weather that matters, utility work.

### 3. Around Town
The human section. A new business opening, a longtime shop's anniversary, a local who did something notable, a photo from a weekend event. **This is the most-forwarded section in every local newsletter.** Never skip it.

### 4. On the Calendar (5-8 items)
What's happening in the next 7-10 days, with day, time, place, and cost. Farmer's Market & Movie Night, Manatee County Fairgrounds events, church and nonprofit events, commission meetings, school events, live music.

### 5. Business Spotlight *(sponsored slot — leave it free early)*
One local business, 100 words, a photo, and an address. Run these free for your first month to build the habit and generate proof for the sales conversation.

### 6. One Last Thing
A closing note — a photo of the sunset over the Green Bridge, a piece of Palmetto history, a reader question. Small, warm, human. This is what makes people stay subscribed.

## Rules that protect the brand

1. **Corrections run in the next issue, at the top, no excuses.** Local trust is the entire asset.
2. **Never run an unverified crime rumor from a Facebook group.** Confirm with PD or don't run it.
3. **Sponsored content is always labeled.** Every time.
4. **Keep church content in its lane.** Church and nonprofit announcements belong in Calendar and Around Town alongside everyone else's. The Palmetto Beat is a community publication, not a ministry publication — that neutrality is what lets it serve the whole town and sell ads to businesses that would never sponsor a ministry newsletter.
