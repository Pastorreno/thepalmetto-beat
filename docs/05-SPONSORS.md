# Monetization — Local Sponsorships

Local newsletters make money one way: **local businesses paying to reach local people.** Not affiliate links, not paid subscriptions, not display ads. Sponsorships.

## The core principle

You are not selling advertising. You are selling **access to the most engaged local audience in Palmetto** — a list of people who chose to receive it and open it at a 50% rate. A Facebook boost reaches strangers. You reach neighbors who read.

## When to start selling

**Not before 500 subscribers.** Below that you can't make a credible case, and a "no" now poisons a lead you'll want later.

**Run free spotlights for your first month.** Feature businesses at no charge. This does three things: builds the habit into the format, gives you a portfolio to show, and means your first sales call opens with *"I featured you last month and here's how many people clicked"* — which is a completely different conversation than a cold pitch.

## Rate card

Set rates once you know your numbers. Rough guide for a local newsletter in a market this size:

| Placement | What it is | 500-1,000 subs | 1,000-2,500 subs | 2,500-5,000 subs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Business Spotlight** | 100 words + photo + address, dedicated section | $75 | $150 | $300 |
| **Presenting Sponsor** | "Presented by [X]" at top, logo, link | $125 | $250 | $500 |
| **Calendar Feature** | Event highlighted at top of Calendar | $40 | $75 | $125 |
| **Classified / Announcement** | 2-3 lines, text only | $25 | $40 | $60 |
| **Monthly package** | Presenting sponsor on all issues in a month | $400 | $800 | $1,600 |

**Always sell monthly packages, not single issues.** One exposure does nothing for a local business and they'll conclude it didn't work. Four weeks of repetition is what produces the "I keep seeing you in The Palmetto Beat" effect that gets renewals. Discount the monthly ~20% against single-issue rates to make it the obvious choice.

## Who to sell to, in order

Rank by who most needs local visibility and has budget:

1. **Real estate agents and brokerages** — highest budget, most local-visibility-hungry. Wagner Realty and similar. Start here.
2. **Home services** — roofing, HVAC, plumbing, pest, lawn. Recurring need, real ad budgets. (Blue Collar Roofing already sponsors local events.)
3. **Restaurants and coffee shops** — smaller budgets, but high enthusiasm and they bring foot traffic proof
4. **Medical / dental / chiropractic** — steady budgets, strictly local patient base
5. **Insurance, mortgage, title** — Revolution Mortgage and Stewart Title already sponsor Palmetto Main Street events, meaning they have a local marketing budget and spend it
6. **Auto dealers and repair**
7. **Banks and credit unions** — slow decisions, bigger checks
8. **Attorneys** — family, estate, personal injury

**Warmest leads:** anyone already sponsoring Palmetto Downtown Main Street events. They've proven they'll pay for local visibility. Stewart Title, Revolution Mortgage, and Blue Collar Roofing are documented Farmer's Market & Movie Night supporters — start your list there.

## The pitch (in person — you live there, use it)

Walk in with a printed copy of a recent issue. Never lead with a cold email.

> "Hi, I'm [name] — I publish The Palmetto Beat, the local newsletter for Palmetto. About [X] people here read it twice a week, and roughly half of them open every issue.
>
> I'm not selling you a big ad campaign. It's a small business spotlight — a hundred words and a photo, so people who already live nearby know you exist. It's $[X] a month.
>
> Can I show you what it looks like?"

Then hand them the printed issue open to a spotlight.

**What closes deals:**
- The printed copy. It makes an email newsletter feel real.
- Your open rate. Say it out loud — 50% is a shocking number to anyone who has run Facebook ads.
- "Your neighbors" framing. Not impressions, not reach. Neighbors.
- Starting small. A $150 test is an easy yes; a $1,600 package is a "let me think about it."

**What kills deals:**
- Leading with price
- Vanity metrics they don't understand
- Selling before you have proof
- Emailing instead of walking in

## The media kit

One page. Bring printed copies. Include:

- What The Palmetto Beat is, one paragraph
- Subscriber count, open rate, click rate — **real numbers only, never inflated.** A local business owner will find out.
- Who reads it: Palmetto and Ellenton residents, homeowners, families, business owners
- Placement options and prices
- One sample issue
- Your name, photo, phone, email

Build this after you have 30 days of real stats. Inventing numbers in a town of 15,000 where everyone knows everyone is not a survivable mistake.

## Sponsorship rules

1. **Always label sponsored content.** "Sponsored" or "Presented by," every time. Losing reader trust destroys the thing you're selling.
2. **Editorial is never for sale.** A sponsor gets their spotlight. They do not get to influence the lead story or kill a story about themselves. Say this out loud in the first conversation — it makes you more credible, not less.
3. **Cap sponsors per issue at two.** More and it stops being a newsletter.
4. **Turn down bad fits.** Payday lenders, MLMs, anything you wouldn't recommend to a neighbor.
5. **Report results.** Send each sponsor their click numbers at month end. This is how renewals happen, and almost no local publisher does it.

## Revenue math

At 2,500 subscribers with two sponsor slots per issue, 8 issues/month:

- 2 monthly packages at $800 = **$1,600/mo**
- 4 calendar features at $75 = **$300/mo**
- Occasional classifieds = **~$150/mo**

**≈ $2,050/month** against roughly $43/month in platform cost.

That's the business. It is not enormous, but it's real, it's durable, it compounds with the list, and it's built on relationships in a town you already live in.
