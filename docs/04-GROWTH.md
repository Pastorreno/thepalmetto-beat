# Growth Plan — First 1,000 Subscribers

You live in Palmetto. That is the entire advantage, and this plan is built to exploit it. Remote operators cannot do most of what follows.

**Realistic ceiling:** Palmetto is roughly 15,000 people, inside the Bradenton–Sarasota metro. Counting Ellenton and the surrounding unincorporated area, a mature list is **3,000–6,000**. At that size, with local sponsorships, this is a genuinely profitable small media business.

## Phase 0 — Before you announce (this week)

- [ ] Register `thepalmettobeat.com` (~$12). Do not announce the name before you own it.
- [ ] Claim `@thepalmettobeat` on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, X, Threads
- [ ] Get a **PO Box in Palmetto** — required for the CAN-SPAM footer, and you don't want your home address in 2,000 inboxes
- [ ] Create a Facebook Page (this is where local audiences actually live)
- [ ] beehiiv publication configured and a test issue sent to yourself

## Phase 1 — First 100 (week 1)

**The warm list.** Everyone who knows you personally. Do NOT bulk-import them — that's how new senders get flagged.

**Do this instead:** send a personal text or message with your signup link. "I'm starting a newsletter about what's happening in Palmetto. Twice a week, five-minute read, free. Would mean a lot if you signed up: [link]"

Individual asks convert 5-10x better than a group post, and everyone who joins this way actively wants it — which is exactly the engagement signal your brand-new sending domain needs.

**Target: 100 subscribers who will open issue #1.** That open rate is what teaches Gmail you're legitimate.

## Phase 2 — First 500 (weeks 2-6) — the in-person phase

This is your unfair advantage. Nobody running a local newsletter remotely can do any of this.

### QR codes on counters
Print a small card: *"Everything happening in Palmetto, in your inbox. Free."* + QR code. Ask to leave a stack at:
- Coffee shops and local restaurants
- The barbershop and salon (people sit and read)
- Local hardware store, feed store, auto shop
- Gyms, the library, City Hall lobby
- Churches (bulletin insert or lobby table)

Ask 20 businesses. Expect 10 yes. **Every one of these is also a future sponsor conversation** — you're prospecting and growing at the same time.

### Show up in person
- **Farmer's Market & Movie Night** (Palmetto Downtown Main Street) — a table, a QR code, and conversation. Highest-density signup opportunity in the city.
- **Manatee County Fairgrounds events** — big regional draw, right in Palmetto
- **City Commission meetings** — attend, introduce yourself, and *publish a recap nobody else writes*. This builds authority fastest.
- **Chamber ribbon cuttings** — show up, take a photo, write them up, hand them a card

### The write-about-them loop
The most reliable local growth engine there is:

> Write about a person or business → tell them it ran → they share it with everyone they know → their audience subscribes.

Feature one local business and one local person **every single issue**. This is not a nice-to-have, it's the growth mechanism.

### Facebook groups — carefully
Local community groups are where Palmetto talks. Do not spam them. Instead: **be genuinely useful.** When someone asks "does anyone know when the road work on 10th ends?" — answer it properly, and mention you cover that in the newsletter. Value first, link second. Ask group admins for permission before ever posting a direct promo.

## Phase 3 — 500 to 1,000 (weeks 6-16)

At 500 subscribers, **upgrade to beehiiv Scale ($43/mo)** and turn on:

### Referral program
Local-flavored rewards beat generic ones:
- **3 referrals** — shoutout by name in the newsletter
- **10 referrals** — Palmetto Beat sticker or koozie
- **25 referrals** — Palmetto Beat t-shirt, plus a Neighbor Spotlight feature
- **50 referrals** — coffee or lunch on you at a local shop you've partnered with

The shoutout at 3 costs nothing and works remarkably well, because people genuinely like seeing their name in the local paper.

### Recommendation network (free — turn on at launch)
Request recommendations from other **Florida / Tampa Bay** newsletters. Local-to-local converts far better than generic swaps. Recommend two or three yourself; reciprocity gets you accepted.

### Cross-promotion with local institutions
Offer a free monthly "community partner" slot to organizations with their own email lists — churches, the library, nonprofits, the chamber. They promote you to their list, you give them a Calendar placement. Pure trade, no cash.

### Local press
Pitch The Bradenton Times or the Business Observer a short piece: *"Why I started a newsletter just for Palmetto."* Local media loves a local media story, and you've already got a hook — nobody covers Palmetto alone.

## What NOT to do

- ❌ **Don't buy a list.** One spam trap can permanently poison your sending reputation.
- ❌ **Don't bulk-import your phone contacts.** The single most common way a new newsletter dies.
- ❌ **Don't run paid ads yet.** Meta ads for a free local newsletter burn money before you know your retention. Revisit after 1,000 organic.
- ❌ **Don't go daily early.** Burnout kills more local newsletters than low subscriber counts do.
- ❌ **Don't chase subscribers outside Palmetto/Ellenton.** A Tampa subscriber is worth nothing to a Palmetto sponsor and drags your engagement rate down.

## Metrics that actually matter

| Metric | Healthy for local | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Open rate** | 40-55% | Local newsletters run far above the 25-30% industry average. Below 35% means your content isn't local enough. |
| **Click rate** | 8-15% | Driven mostly by the Calendar section |
| **Unsubscribe** | under 0.5%/issue | Above 1% means wrong audience or wrong cadence |
| **Spam complaints** | under 0.1% | Above 0.3% and Gmail starts filtering you |
| **Growth** | 10-15%/month early | Slower is fine if engagement is high |

Open rate is the number to defend. A 3,000-subscriber list at 50% open is worth far more to a local sponsor than 8,000 at 20% — and it's what you'll put on the rate card.
